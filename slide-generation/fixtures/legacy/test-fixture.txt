Our campus library has a simple problem: getting a book takes too long, even when we already know exactly which book we need.

We tested a pickup service for two weeks. Students reserved a book online, library staff collected it from the shelves, and the student picked it up from a locker. So the process was reserve, collect, then pick up. Three steps, with no searching the shelves yourself.

The pilot involved sixty students. Actually, let me correct that: sixty signed up, but forty-eight completed the trial. The results I'm describing are from those forty-eight.

The biggest improvement was time. With the usual process, getting a book took twenty minutes on average. With locker pickup, it took five minutes. That's fifteen minutes saved per collection, or a seventy-five percent reduction.

There were two other improvements. Successful first visits increased from eighty percent to ninety-five percent. And student satisfaction rose from three point two to four point six out of five.

Sorry, that last number was four point four, not four point six. Three point two before, four point four after.

There is a trade-off. Staff spent an additional thirty minutes each day preparing the reserved books. So this saved students time, but it wasn't free of extra work.

Our recommendation is to extend the pilot for one month, keeping it limited to high-demand textbooks. The main takeaway is faster access to books, with a staffing cost we still need to evaluate.
